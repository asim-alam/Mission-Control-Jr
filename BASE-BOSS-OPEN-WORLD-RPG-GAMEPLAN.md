# Mission Control Jr. — Base Boss: The Meridian Network

**Game design and production plan · 12 November 2026**  
**Audience** — Ages 7–12 · Single-player 3D browser RPG · Working title

## Product decision

Give the player command of a small region on Mars. They can leave the control room, meet the people who need help, load a rover, choose a route, and return to a base that has changed because of their decision. The full game has five interconnected communities, one 30-sol campaign, and a free-play period after the handover. A sol is a day on Mars. A community here is a small crew and its workplace, such as a greenhouse or relay station. The setting is a fictional later expedition built around NASA planning constraints.

**The scene to build the game around** — A young commander has enough power to charge a rover or keep a greenhouse on schedule. They choose how to deliver a water-recycler part, see the pumps and lights react, then return a few sols later to see what happened to the crop. The strongest competition entry puts that complete chain in a judge’s hands within four minutes.

| Decision | Direction |
|---|---|
| Player role | Act as the junior commander under Reyes. Explore in third person, talk to the crews, personally drive or dispatch a rover, and make regional decisions at a shared mission table. |
| Open-world scope | One continuous, authored Mars region with short traverses between recognizable places. The region grows as crews commission facilities, repair routes and establish supply links. |
| What makes it an RPG | Persistent characters remember commitments. The player earns capabilities through work, changes relationships and facilities through choices, and can pursue different combinations of science, resilience and service. |
| Time | Planning, reading, free exploration around a base and accessibility interactions pause operational time. Committed travel, work, waiting and end-of-shift actions advance it. Manual driving and assisted travel use the same route ledger. |
| Delivery | Keep Three.js and Vite. Ship through a direct browser link, with keyboard and touch controls, local resume and downloadable source records available from the debrief. |
| Release scope | Build one polished, replayable delivery first. Grow the regional network after it passes child playtests. The full design assumes fictional NPC crews and a local single-player save. |

## Current game audit

I opened the running browser build at `127.0.0.1:5173`, chose **Base Boss · 30 Days**, and played through the opening event on Sol 3. I inspected the rest of the mode in source. I did not play all 30 sols.

- On Sol 1, **Look after the plants** advertised a cost of 4 power and 3 water. By Sol 2, after daily upkeep, power had moved from 70 to 60 and water from 70 to 62. The log promised food later. The player sees meaningful delayed consequences, but must work out which changes came from their action versus routine consumption.
- I chose **Make air and clean water**, then paid 10 power to test the greenhouse mushroom in the Sol 3 event. The log scheduled results for Sol 21. The camera shows a habitat, solar panels, a rover and astronauts, while the actions happen in a bottom button panel and event dialogs. The crew and terrain give the setting a face, but the opening provides no way to visit a station or use the rover.
- The mode already has four named crew, seeded event outcomes, daily upkeep, delayed crops, distinct dust and solar-storm events, earlier-mission carryover and an end-of-run decision review. The old rover is a narrative event. The main command is one daily job for the whole crew. Seven resource bars, four crew summaries and a log compete for attention.
- Retire the mushroom event for the RPG. Use a known, planted crop with a failing pump. The current event already has distinct test, eat and discard branches, with a Sol 21 follow-up gated to test or eat. Keep that branching structure and shorten the feedback loop. Show the causal chain in the debrief.

## The region and its people

Put Meridian in a shallow basin. From the hub window, children can recognize Verdant by its greenhouse ribs, Kestrel by the relay mast, Forge by its striped gantry and Pathfinder by its low shelter beside the exposed strata. Begin with Meridian and Verdant plus an uncrewed landing cache. The full release adds three staffed sites and secondary connections.

| Community | What it supplies | What it needs | How it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meridian Hub** · Reyes and Luca | Dispatch, steady power at the hub, medical support, emergency reserves and shielded beds | Reliable parts, food deliveries and access to the relay | The regional board fills with working routes, and restored facilities send status reports from the world. |
| **Verdant Greenhouse** · Amara | Fresh produce, seed stock and growing trials | Water, lighting, filters and scheduled maintenance | Live trays go from seedlings to harvest. Recovery can reopen the water loop before the next crop is ready. |
| **Forge Depot** · Jin and the cargo crew | Refurbished spares, battery charging and freight capacity | Prepositioned stock, work shifts and power | Fix the loader and a heavy route becomes serviceable. Recycling a used component frees a slot for another delivery. |
| **Kestrel Relay** · communications crew | Local radio coverage, Earth message uplink and route reports | A working mast, service batteries and occasional parts | Relay lights and radio chatter return. A repaired repeater enables escorted dispatch across the far side of the ridge. |
| **Pathfinder Field Camp** · survey crew | Terrain surveys, dust sensors, geology samples and updated route markers | Packed food, power packs, shelter access and pickup slots | Surveyed paths are added to the planner. Sample collection uses sealed containers and designated routes. |

The network creates reciprocal dependencies. Forge sends a filter to Verdant. Verdant sends fresh produce back on the return leg. Pathfinder surveys a route that lowers the cost of that loop. Kestrel lets the crews coordinate dispatch on it. If Forge loses a charger, the route still exists but a convoy may need a detour or smaller load. Each location needs an optional story, a return visit with changed dialogue, and a practical reason to revisit.

Use a small cast and give each person a recognizable responsibility and a competing priority. Reyes wants a safe handover. Amara wants to keep an experiment alive. Jin wants to avoid exhausting repair stock. Luca protects rest and shelter capacity. Trust comes from fulfilling commitments or communicating a change early. It unlocks favors such as lending a cart, sharing a cache or covering a shift. Keep emergency assistance available regardless of trust.

## What the player does

1. **Hear the need.** Walk to a person, use a radio call, or open the mission table. A request shows what is needed, where, by when and what happens if it slips.
2. **Make a plan.** Pick cargo, a route and a crew assignment. The game previews charge on arrival and return, the safety reserve, deadline and what the action displaces back at base. Select one commitment in Cadet. Explorer can schedule parallel work and combine deliveries.
3. **Carry it out.** Pick up the marked crate, see it click into the rack, follow landmarks and unload at the matching socket. Drive personally, use assisted steering, or dispatch by map. All three options obey the same cargo, travel, route and resource rules.
4. **See the result.** A repaired pump turns on, a crew member returns to work, a sheltered team misses an outdoor task, or an arrival leaves enough charge to help another base. The timeline records the chosen action, immediate cost and later effect.
5. **Set the next priority.** Change course as forecasts and crew requests change. Unlock new routes and capabilities through completed work, then apply them to a problem the player has already seen.

**Cargo and rovers.** Start with one electric utility rover, two cargo slots and a visible return reserve. A crate has one location and one destination. Loading reserves the item and slots, travel spends charge, unloading moves the item to the receiving inventory, and installation spends crew time. Unclaimed cargo remains where it was left. Route cards show travel time, charge, clearance, current hazards and the next refuge. Later add an autonomous hauler for mapped repeat routes and a pressurized expedition rover that trades charging time and payload space for safer long excursions. A disabled rover keeps its cargo and gets a tow, recovery beacon or emergency return. The detour costs time and supplies.

**Systems and consequences.** Each site stores power, breathable-air and water reserves, food, spares and shelter capacity. The mission table shows **Power**, **Air & Water** and **Shelter**. Food gets its own contextual card in the greenhouse or when stocks run low. Wear, fatigue and detailed reserves appear where they inform a decision. Life support consumes power each shift. A broken recycler draws down its local reserves until repaired. A planted crop advances only when its needs are met. Forecasts distinguish **dust reducing solar output** from **solar particles increasing radiation exposure**. Shelter and recall reduce modeled exposure. An alert stops outdoor work or diverts a rover into a refuge. Use training categories such as “inside,” “outside” and “safer shelter”; keep scored exposure separate from real dose units. A protected reserve can buy recovery time. Continued shortages can force an early safe handover and a replay from the decision point.

**Clock and local autonomy.** Use three operational shifts per sol. Every committed action shows its cost in shifts or part of a shift. After committing an action, advance the simulation to the next scheduled event or to the action’s end. A relay issue queues messages, while Earth light-time applies even with a working relay. A status card tells the child what they can do safely while waiting. Background tabs and closed sessions do not advance the clock.

## First playable quest — One Crate Before Dark

**Start state.** Verdant’s water recycler has a failing valve, enough stored water to stay safe today, and a crop close to harvest. A pre-landed cache holds the spare. Dust will cut Verdant’s solar production next shift. Its safety reserve is protected, but greenhouse lights and local charging need a choice. Meridian’s steady supply can charge a rover for a cost to its own work plan.

| Option | Preview | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge at Meridian and take the marked route** | Spend 4 spare grid units to move rover charge from 12 to 16. Route costs 10 round trip, leaving 6 against a reserve of 4. Meridian’s sample analyzer waits. | The valve arrives this shift, water recovers before the next one, and Verdant’s crop stays on schedule. The research slot at Meridian slips. |
| **Use the slower route and save the charge** | Use the existing 12 units. Easier terrain costs 8 round trip, leaving the reserve of 4. Arrive one shift later. Verdant uses stored water for the crew and pauses its lights. | Meridian keeps its research slot. The valve still arrives safely, and Verdant’s harvest slips a shift. Packed food covers the gap. |

The preview uses authored training units. These numbers are starting tuning values. The 4 units moved to the rover use a simplified lossless transfer. The research and harvest gains belong to different priorities, so each plan should win on a different outcome. If the player explores the cache area first, they can discover the easier route through a marker or ask Jin to show it on the map. Both options remain accessible from the planner.

**Walkthrough for judging.** Within 30 seconds the player sees Amara’s request and the route map. In the next minute they compare the plans and load a crate. Within four minutes they arrive, install the valve, see the pump restart and get the crop forecast. The debrief shows what the player preserved, what they delayed and why dust affected Verdant. **Try the other route** restores the same starting forecast, cargo and charge. The sample scene covers power, water, food and logistics. Add the separate solar-particle shelter and Earth-delay scenarios to the full challenge entry.

## Campaign, quests and endings

| Period | Main story | Branches and optional work |
|---|---|---|
| **Sols 1–4 · Take Command** | Learn the map and bring Verdant its valve. Commit the first shift and see the next shift’s consequence. | Survey a return route, use an assisted delivery, or preserve Meridian’s research slot. |
| **Sols 5–10 · Open the Network** | Commission Forge’s loader, connect Kestrel and send a first message to Earth. A repair has to proceed under a local checklist while the response is in transit. | Make a paired delivery, set up a spare cache, or spend a shift extending local radio coverage. |
| **Sols 11–17 · Dust Front** | A historical dust profile lowers solar yield at remote sites over several shifts. Choose where limited mobile storage and rover time go. | Carry a battery to a remote site, dispatch a crew to restore its feeder, or put the greenhouse in a low-power schedule and consume packed food. |
| **Sols 18–24 · A Place to Shelter** | A separate solar-particle forecast reaches the network during a Pathfinder survey. Compare returning early to Meridian, using a nearby qualified refuge with fewer beds, or moving a freight task to free up shelter capacity. | Preposition supplies, expand refuge capacity, or move science indoors. A rover used as refuge must be designated and equipped for the scenario. |
| **Sols 25–30 · Handover** | Close the rotation by making the network ready for the next crew. Select a final priority and see the consequence of cumulative infrastructure and supply decisions. | Bring home samples, finish the fresh-food loop, or complete a backup route and emergency cache. |

Ship a core chain plus roughly two meaningful optional quests per community in the full release. Each quest needs a requestor, a genuine opportunity cost, a world change, a recovery route and a short debrief. Keep a player-chosen objective pinned. Other requests go on the board and can expire into an explained fallback. Essential events should forecast their deadlines. Rest and recovery remain possible between peaks.

Endings use observable criteria. **Network Ready** leaves every crew safe with staffed refuges and viable supply routes. **Safe Handover** protects people but leaves selected services pending. **Recovery Rotation** uses emergency assistance and describes the decision that could avoid it on replay. Record optional achievements for science, fresh-food delivery and spare capacity independently. After a safe handover, let the player continue trading cargo and exploring in the same world. Replay a chapter from a checkpoint to compare decisions while preserving the main save.

## Visual and interaction direction

Build one memorable shot before expanding the map. From inside Meridian’s command room, the player sees the landed cache across the basin. On the rover ride, terrain hides Verdant until its lit greenhouse comes over a rise. Use believable hardware and warm, inviting interiors. Add physical details that explain play: numbered crates, charge cables, scuffed floor lanes, storage racks and a valve socket that matches the crate. Give each facility its own shape, sound and lighting. Signs and icons reinforce color cues.

The normal screen has one objective, one in-world marker and one contextual action. Open the mission table for comparing routes and resources. Use a diegetic station for loading and fixing equipment, backed by accessible HTML controls. Every consequential interaction should show a world response and a concise line such as “Water is flowing. The crop will be ready one shift later.” Sound cues and captions carry the same information. Provide an adjustable camera, reduced motion, repeatable voice lines, high-contrast markers and a one-button map route.

**Cadet, ages 7–9** — Show two plans at a time, combine air and water into one status, preview consequences with text and icons, offer read-aloud and default to assisted driving. **Explorer, ages 10–12** — Reveal individual stocks, route costs, cargo combinations, work queues and historical records on demand. Both modes use the same scenario rules. All critical choices are keyboard operable, with visible focus and roomy touch targets. Every driving task has a map-based equivalent. Teach the word “sol” the first time it appears.

## Production sequence and release gates

NASA’s event is scheduled for **14–15 November 2026**. The full RPG is a post-event production scope. For the submission, use the current playable build as the fallback. Put the new delivery in front of judges only if the complete route, consequence and replay work in a fresh browser. Pair it with the existing Solar Shelter and Earth Delay scenarios if the integrated chapter is still in production, and identify which parts belong to the current build. **Browser targets** — First playable state within five seconds on a 20 Mbps connection with a cold cache, first route’s initial download at or below 6 MB compressed, and sustained 30 fps at 720p on the team’s target low-end laptop with low graphics. Measure in Chrome and Firefox on the target laptop and test touch on an Android phone. Check keyboard-only flow, focus, text scaling, map fallback, resuming a saved delivery and the last shift of the campaign. These are proposed targets, with hardware and measured results still to be established.

**Learning targets** — In small moderated sessions covering both age bands, aim for 4 of 5 children in each band to make the first choice without adult instruction within a minute and explain a specific causal consequence after replay. Ask “What did you give up?” and “What would you do if another crew needed water?” Record where they hesitate, which resources they confuse, and whether they choose to try a second route. Review reachability of both successful plans with the same weather seed, then vary the forecast to test for a dominant strategy.

**Team priority** — Assign simulation and logistics to one owner, environment and rover presentation to another, and quest writing, interface and child testing to a third role as staffing allows. The first integration review should be a fresh-link playthrough with the actual simulated cargo and time costs. The competitive opportunity is a child who can point to a working greenhouse and explain which decision made it possible.
