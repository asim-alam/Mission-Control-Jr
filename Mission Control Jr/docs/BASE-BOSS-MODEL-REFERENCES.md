# Base Boss · NASA hardware and site references

The five sites belong to a fictional later Mars expedition. The region uses the repository’s HiRISE Jezero height sample rescaled into a playable basin. Site pads, landmarks, distances, travel speeds and mission budgets are authored. The submitted screenshots informed the dusty light, vehicle tracks, equipment spacing and large landforms. They are visual references for the game.

NASA’s **Moon to Mars Architecture Definition Document**, Revision C, §2.1.4 and Appendix C, supplies the planning framework. Mars assets are still being defined. Use the 2009 DRA and 2022 surface activity study for particular illustrations and operating examples, with their dates and concept status attached. [NASA architecture library](https://www.nasa.gov/architecture-definition/) · [Revision C](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/add-revision-c-20251211.pdf) · [NASA HEOMD-415](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000589/downloads/MarsSAC21SurfaceOps_2022-Jan_Version%201%20FINAL_update.pdf)

## Regional layout in the browser

From Meridian’s basin floor, head west to Forge, north to the Kestrel ridge, east along the ridge to Pathfinder, and south to Verdant. The southern cache provides two connections to Verdant: a steeper service cut and a longer, firmer wash. Their travel time and power costs differ. A perimeter route lets players return via a different base. Marked stops provide parking, a quest contact and recharging. Base-to-base travel is scaled for short sessions.

The browser map and rover use one terrain and route definition. Free driving consumes charge and advances simulation time with distance. Assisted travel follows surveyed routes with the same terrain-dependent costs. Quests cover recycler spares, food, a delayed Earth reply, a geology return and shelter supplies. Any charge shortfall can be recovered by a tow to the last safe stop. The valve quest compresses the archived Opportunity array-energy decline into 20 mission minutes. Verdant’s light threshold and the crop delay are authored. See the in-game Sources panel for the historical dust, light-time and terrain records. [Jezero HiRISE DTM](https://hirise.lpl.arizona.edu/dtm/ESP_023524_1985) · [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) · [2018 dust storm observations](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia22737-mars-climate-sounder-studies-2018-dust-storm/)

## Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer drill

Select **Run the outpost drill** in Base Boss. Three ten-minute training shifts let a player allocate power, shelter the crew and decide how to handle a delayed Earth reply. Drill progress is independent of rover jobs. The results panel records the effect of each choice and allows replay from the same starting conditions.

| Decision | NASA basis | In-game rule |
|---|---|---|
| Allocate electricity to air, water and crops | NASA’s ISS life-support description connects water recovery and oxygen generation. The 2018 Opportunity archive provides the shape of the solar output decline. | Limited feeder power plus solar generation and battery storage serve the selected loads. The air system has first priority, clean water is finite, and the crop needs water and light. [ECLSS](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/) · [Opportunity](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/opportunity-hunkers-down-during-dust-storm/) |
| Choose a refuge | Curiosity recorded a major radiation surge during the separate May 2024 solar particle event. | The reading anchors 100 outdoor exposure points. The two modeled shelters reduce exposure by authored amounts. Sealed water held in the extra shielding is temporarily unavailable to the crew. [Curiosity RAD event](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-watches-mars-light-up-during-epic-solar-storm/) |
| Keep the crop going | OSD-269 documents a 16-hour light and 8-hour dark lettuce protocol aboard the ISS. | Two successful light-and-water cycles finish an already-grown training crop. Packed food remains the fallback. [OSD-269](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/data/studies/OSD-269) |
| Act while a reply is in transit | A bundled JPL Horizons row gives about 11.01 minutes of one-way Earth–Mars light time on 14 November 2026. | Earth’s reply arrives after roughly 22.03 mission minutes with zero ground processing time in the drill. The local checklist costs power. Waiting spends stored air and changes the final power allocation. [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) |

The reactor stays on in this scenario. The outage is a local limit on the electricity delivered to Verdant. The game’s dose index, operating loads, water and crop rates are educational abstractions. The scenario combines records from separate dates, with each record identified in the drill.
