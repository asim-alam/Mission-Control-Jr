# Mission Control Jr. learning objectives

This prototype uses language aimed at learners approximately 10–14 years old. The official challenge describes students but does not specify a precise age range. Game values and rates are deliberately simplified and are not operational mission-planning data.

## What players practice

1. **Power and storms:** sunlight powers solar arrays; airborne dust can reduce available solar energy. Storage and reduced loads can help bridge a low-power period.
2. **Connected life support:** air, water, food production and power are linked systems. A choice that protects one resource can reduce another.
3. **Multi-use mass:** water is a vital supply and water-rich mass can contribute to radiation protection in a shelter design.
4. **Radiation planning:** protection depends on shielding and warning time; a crew should move to a prepared shelter during a solar-particle event.
5. **Safe traverse planning:** compare terrain evidence, route cost and a return reserve; revise plans when the observed ground differs from the map.
6. **Crew autonomy:** Mars communication delays mean a crew cannot depend on real-time Earth guidance for every decision.
7. **Evidence before interpretation:** record observable features and uncertainty before drawing a scientific conclusion.

## NASA and partner-agency references

- NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, [Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/build-a-junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/): the challenge asks learners to balance outpost life support, radiation shielding, power and food production.
- NASA/JPL, [Mars rovers braving severe dust storms](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-mars-rovers-braving-severe-dust-storms/): dust storms reduced solar-panel output and controllers conserved energy by suspending activities.
- NASA Science, [Solar-panel dust accumulation and cleanings](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/solar-panel-dust-accumulation-and-cleanings/): deposited dust can reduce sunlight reaching solar arrays; wind can sometimes clear it.
- NASA, [MOXIE](https://www.nasa.gov/space-technology-mission-directorate/tdm/mars-oxygen-in-situ-resource-utilization-experiment-moxie/): the technology demonstration extracts oxygen from carbon dioxide in Mars' atmosphere.
- NASA, [Real Martians: how to protect astronauts from space radiation on Mars](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/real-martians-how-to-protect-astronauts-from-space-radiation-on-mars/): discusses candidate materials and shielding concepts.
- NASA, [Mars communications disruption and delay](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/mars-communications-disruption-and-delay.pdf): Mars mission delay and blackout conditions require substantial crew and system autonomy.
- NASA/JPL Robotics, [Surface system software and rover navigation](https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/what-we-do/flight-projects/mars-science-laboratory/surface-system-software-and-rover-navigation/): rover planners assess terrain images and choose safe routes.
- NASA Science, [How Perseverance drives on Mars](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/how-perseverance-drives-on-mars/): rover drivers use terrain imagery and 3D views to plan drives.
