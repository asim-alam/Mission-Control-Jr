# Mission Control Jr. — 4-minute voice-over

**Target duration: 04:00 exactly**

### 0:00–0:12 | Opening hook

*[Visual: Mars dust storm. Cut to a game resource panel showing limited power. A short pause before the question.]*

A dust storm is approaching Mars. Solar power is falling. The crew has one reserve battery. What should a junior astronaut protect first?

### 0:12–0:27 | Team and project

*[Visual: Mission Control Jr. title. Bongo Aphelion team card appears with all current team names and roles.]*

We are Bongo Aphelion, and this is Mission Control Jr., created for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer.

### 0:27–0:50 | WHY: the learning gap

*[Visual: Oxygen, water, power and food icons connect. Show two competing resource choices.]*

The challenge is more than remembering space facts. It asks children to manage an outpost where power, water, food, communication and crew safety interact. What happens when you fix one problem but spend the energy needed for another? We want children to experience that decision.

### 0:50–1:15 | WHAT: the experience

*[Visual: Mission map. Highlight Mars, Moon, Cadet, Explorer and Be the Base Boss.]*

Mission Control Jr. is a three-dimensional browser game with six Mars chapters, three Moon missions and a separate Be the Base Boss experience: ten selectable experiences in total. Players arrive, repair, explore, protect a crew, grow food, manage delayed messages and connect distant outposts. Cadet and Explorer settings support different age groups.

### 1:15–2:35 | HOW: gameplay demonstration

*[Visual: Use real gameplay captures. Keep the player's action and the resulting feedback visible.]*

In Arrival at Mars, players guide a spacecraft into a careful docking, repair a radio and prepare to land.

On the surface, Before the Storm asks them to clean solar collectors, recover a spare battery and reconnect the power system. Their next choice balances life-support reserve against continued science.

In Tracks in the Dust, they drive a rover, compare routes, inspect uncertain terrain, capture photographs and keep enough energy for the return.

A Place to Hide asks them to arrange shielding materials and bring the crew to shelter.

The Greenhouse Promise turns food production into a system check: support plant light and water, diagnose airflow and decide what can be harvested.

In Message to Earth, players must make a plan while a reply travels across space.

The Moon campaign adds solar-mast placement, dust-control tasks and a communication relay beyond a ridge.

Finally, Be the Base Boss opens the wider Meridian Network, connecting six outposts through routes, deliveries and training drills.

### 2:35–3:15 | NASA data and scientific context

*[Visual: Show the four data cards: Opportunity, HiRISE, OSD-269 and Horizons. Cut between a NASA source and the corresponding game scene.]*

NASA sources support the science behind these missions. Opportunity's historical solar-energy record shows a fall from six hundred forty-five to twenty-two watt-hours per sol during the 2018 dust storm.

A small NASA HiRISE elevation sample from Jezero shapes the rover terrain.

The greenhouse references NASA's OSD-269 lettuce study and its sixteen-hour light, eight-hour dark schedule.

JPL Horizons supplies archived Earth-to-Mars light-time values for the delayed message.

Other NASA records inform the shelter and lunar missions. These are archived observations and references, not live data feeds. Game budgets, crop yields, rates and some geometry remain simplified training values.

### 3:15–3:45 | Educational value

*[Visual: Pip tip, read-aloud button, Simple and Details modes, mission objective, badge and debrief.]*

Designed for learners aged seven to twelve, the game offers Cadet and Explorer settings, Pip's short tips, read-aloud support, and Simple or Details explanations.

Players practise systems thinking, resource planning, navigation, careful observation and problem-solving. Mission debriefs help them compare results and replay decisions.

These are our intended learning goals. Supervised playtesting and educational evaluation are still needed before we claim measured learning gains.
