# **Mission Control Jr.: Session 1, Meridian: Mission to Mars**

*Game design and script*

*A game by Bongo Aphelion, NASA Space Apps Bangladesh*

## **1\. Session Overview**

**Session 1** follows a crew through a complete Mars mission, from the first day of training to the splashdown back on Earth. The heart of the game is running the outpost: balancing life support, radiation shielding, power, and food (see Section 2). The player doesn’t just survive on Mars. They prepare for the trip, learn the spaceship, launch it, fly it across space, live on Mars, and bring everyone home.

The session has **six levels**. Each level has **sub-levels**, and each sub-level has **missions** and **challenges**. Every level can be played on **three difficulty modes**.

*Game time note:* A real Mars mission takes about three years (roughly seven months each way, plus time on Mars). The game compresses this. Travel is played as a series of key days, and the Mars stay is 30 sols (a sol is one Martian day).

### **The Levels**

| Level | Name | What the player does |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | **Preparation** | Train to become a Mars astronaut |
| 2 | **Know Your Spaceship** | Learn the ship’s systems and pack it |
| 3 | **Launch** | Run the countdown and leave Earth |
| 4 | **The Long Journey** | Fly seven months to Mars and survive the trip |
| 5 | **Survive on Mars** | Land, build, and run the outpost for 30 sols |
| 6 | **The Way Back Home** | Leave Mars, rendezvous, and return to Earth |

### **The Crew**

A crew of **five**, each with their own role (see Section 5):

* **Tomas Reyes**, commander. Calm in a crisis, but he rarely sleeps.

* **Kenji Watanabe**, mechanical engineer. He can fix almost anything.

* **Dr. Amara Okafor**, medical officer. She notices stress before anyone admits it.

* **Lucia Moreno**, botanist. She talks to her plants.

* **Priya Nair**, pilot. Steady hands, and a dry sense of humor.

Two voices support them:

* **Capcom Jordan Hale**, voice of Mission Control on Earth. Patient, funny, never panics on the radio.

* **Compass**, the ship’s computer. It speaks plainly and always gives the numbers.

The player is the **mission planner**, making decisions for the crew.

## **2\. Core Challenge: Run the Outpost**

**This is the main focus of the game.** Everything else in Session 1 supports it.

### **The Challenge**

Space-themed learning content often goes wrong in one of two ways. It oversimplifies the engineering trade-offs that decide a real mission, or it explains them at a level too complex for a young learner to enjoy. Very few tools make those trade-offs both tangible and fun.

Our game answers this. Students run a **Martian outpost** (Session 1\) or a **lunar outpost** (Session 2\) and balance the competing demands that decide whether a mission fails or succeeds:

* **Life support** (oxygen, water, temperature)

* **Radiation shielding**

* **Power**

* **Food production**

Students don’t read about trade-offs. They feel them. Power given to the greenhouse isn’t available for the shield, and the game shows the result, one sol later.

### **The Four Competing Demands**

| Demand | What it needs | What it competes with | Real engineering fact | In the game |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Life support** | Power to make oxygen and clean water | Greenhouse, shield | Space crews recycle most of their water because shipping it from Earth is very costly | Oxygen and water bars fall every sol and rise when funded |
| **Radiation shielding** | Power and upkeep to repair | Everything else | Mars has thin air and no global magnetic field, so shielding matters, and the Moon has no air at all | The shield wears down daily, and flares damage it |
| **Power** | Sunlight, storage, and good planning | Nothing, but it limits everything | Dust storms and the lunar night can cut solar power for days or weeks | Power units per sol, with a battery that carries over |
| **Food production** | Power, light, water, and time | Life support, shield | Food grown on site matters because resupply takes months | Greenhouse output turns power into food, slowly |

Two secondary demands keep the board interesting: **equipment wear** (machines break down) and **crew morale** (tired crews make mistakes).

### **How the Trade-Off Engine Works**

1. **One shared pool.** Each sol, the crew has a fixed number of power units. The player splits them across the demands.

2. **Everything decays.** Air, water, food, and shielding all fall each sol. Doing nothing is a choice with consequences.

3. **Forecasts, not surprises.** Each sol starts with a forecast (storm, flare, leak). Players plan against known risks, which is how real mission planners work.

4. **Clear cause and effect.** After each sol, a short **Mission Report** explains what happened and why. For example: “The shield fell below 40, so the crew absorbed radiation.”

5. **Fail or succeed, understandably.** A failed mission always shows which decision chain led to it, and a one-click replay lets the student try a different path.

6. **Short-term versus long-term.** Research and upgrades pay off later but cost power today. This is the hardest and most realistic trade-off in the game.

### **Two Learner Levels**

The game is built for both **Beginner/Youth** and **Intermediate** learners.

|  | Beginner/Youth | Intermediate |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Interface | Big sliders, simple icons, plain-language hints | Numbers, rates, and units shown |
| Explanations | One short sentence per event | Short sentence plus the engineering reason |
| Complexity | Four demands | Four demands plus equipment, morale, and dose |
| Maps to difficulty | Cadet | Astronaut and Commander |

### **How Each Level Serves the Core Challenge**

| Level | Role in the core challenge |
| :---- | :---- |
| 1\. Preparation | Mini-games teach each demand one at a time before they combine |
| 2\. Know Your Spaceship | The ship has its own power, air, and shielding trade-offs, plus a weight budget |
| 3\. Launch | Every kilogram of supplies costs fuel, which is the first big trade-off |
| 4\. The Long Journey | A small version of the outpost on the move, with tight resources and no resupply |
| **5\. Survive on Mars** | **The full outpost game. This is the heart of the experience** |
| 6\. The Way Back Home | Everything is worn out, so the trade-offs are tougher than ever |

## **3\. Other Challenges**

Besides running the outpost, students can take on **side challenges**. They sit between levels and can also be played on their own.

| Challenge | What the student does | Skill practiced |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Shield Builder** | Design a radiation shield from water, soil, and plastic within a mass limit | Engineering trade-offs (protection versus weight) |
| **Night Planner** | Size batteries and panels to survive a long lunar night or a dust storm | Power planning |
| **Greenhouse Optimizer** | Choose crops for the most food from limited light, water, and space | Systems thinking |
| **Air and Water Balance** | Keep gas levels stable by finding the failing filter | Troubleshooting |
| **Cargo Crunch** | Load a lander under a fixed weight limit | Budgeting |
| **Emergency Response** | Handle a fire, leak, or power failure against a countdown | Fast decisions |
| **Data Detective** | Read real NASA data, such as dust opacity on Mars or radiation readings, and decide what it means for the base | Data literacy |
| **Daily Challenge** | A new random scenario each day with a shared leaderboard | Replay value |
| **Classroom Mode** | A teacher sets difficulty and events, and the class compares results | Group learning |
| **Sandbox** | Free play with custom settings for experimenting | Curiosity |

**Real examples used in challenges:** \- NASA’s MOXIE experiment on the Perseverance rover made oxygen from Martian carbon dioxide. \- Curiosity’s radiation detector measured the radiation a crew would face on Mars. \- The Opportunity rover’s mission ended after a global dust storm in 2018 blocked its sunlight.

### **Match to the Challenge Brief**

| What the challenge asks | How the game delivers it |
| :---- | :---- |
| An interactive game or app | A playable browser game with levels, story, and challenges |
| Run a lunar or Martian outpost | Mars in Session 1 and the Moon in Session 2 |
| Balance life support, shielding, power, and food | The four demands share one power pool each sol |
| Make trade-offs tangible | Visible bars, Mission Reports, and a replay option |
| Make trade-offs fun | Story, a five-person crew with distinct roles, events, stars, and badges |
| Let students feel what makes a mission fail or succeed | Clear fail and success endings, with the reasons shown |
| Suit a young learner’s attention | Short sub-levels, plain language, and a Beginner/Youth mode |

## **4\. Difficulty Modes**

| Setting | Cadet (Easy) | Astronaut (Normal) | Commander (Hard) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Resource use | Slower | Standard | Faster |
| Event forecasts | Always shown, with hints | Shown at the start of the day | Shown only some days |
| Random events | Fewer | Standard | More, and they can chain |
| Mistakes | Can be undone once per level | Cannot be undone | Have lasting effects |
| Hints | Unlimited | 3 per level | None |
| Time limits | Relaxed or off | Standard | Strict |
| Crew stress | Builds slowly | Standard | Builds quickly |
| Stars needed to unlock next level | 1 | 2 | 3 |

**Stars.** Each level awards up to 3 stars: one for finishing, one for a safe crew (nobody badly hurt), and one for a bonus goal (like saving fuel or finishing a side mission).

**Ranks.** Stars earn ranks: Cadet, Pilot, Flight Engineer, Mission Specialist, Commander.

## **5\. Crew Roles**

The crew has **five roles**, and every mission carries all five. Each role has its own skills, challenges, and story moments, so no one is just a background character.

| Role | Crew member | Main job |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Commander | Tomas Reyes | Leads the crew and makes the final calls |
| Mechanical Engineer | Kenji Watanabe | Keeps machines, pumps, power, and the hull working |
| Medical Officer (Doctor) | Dr. Amara Okafor | Keeps everyone healthy, in body and mind |
| Botanist | Lucia Moreno | Grows food and runs the greenhouse |
| Pilot | Priya Nair | Flies the ship, lands it, and docks it |

### **Role Cards**

Each role has a **passive bonus** that is always active, a **special skill** that can be used once per level, and a **role challenge** only that role can run.

**Commander (Tomas Reyes)** \- *Passive:* Crew morale falls more slowly. \- *Special skill:* “Rally the Crew” restores morale after a bad event. \- *Role challenge (Dilemma):* Make a hard call with incomplete information, such as go or no-go before launch, or whether to shelter from a storm.

**Mechanical Engineer (Kenji Watanabe)** \- *Passive:* Equipment wears out more slowly. \- *Special skill:* “Quick Fix” repairs a broken system using no spare parts. \- *Role challenge (Puzzle):* Find the faulty part in a pump, valve, wiring panel, or airlock by testing in the right order.

**Medical Officer (Dr. Amara Okafor)** \- *Passive:* Injuries and illness heal faster. \- *Special skill:* “Full Check-Up” reveals every crew member’s hidden health and stress levels. \- *Role challenge (Dilemma):* Triage. Two crew members need help and you can treat only one first.

**Botanist (Lucia Moreno)** \- *Passive:* The greenhouse makes a little more food. \- *Special skill:* “Fast Harvest” produces a one-time burst of fresh food. \- *Role challenge (Budget):* Decide which crops to plant given limited light, water, and space.

**Pilot (Priya Nair)** \- *Passive:* Landings and dockings use less fuel. \- *Special skill:* “Steady Hands” gives extra time on a timing challenge. \- *Role challenge (Timing):* Land, dock, or burn at exactly the right moment.

### **Crew Rules**

* **Stress meter.** Every crew member has one. A role used too often without rest is more likely to make mistakes.

* **Down for the day.** An exhausted or injured crew member can’t use their special skill. Another crew member can cover at half strength, which teaches why teams need backup.

* **Synergies.** Some pairs work better together. Mechanical Engineer plus Pilot saves fuel on burns and repairs. Doctor plus Commander keeps morale steady. Botanist plus Doctor gives the crew better meals and better health.

* **Cross-training.** In Level 1, any astronaut can learn a second role’s special skill, but only at half strength.

### **Role Spotlight by Level**

| Level | Roles in the spotlight and what they do |
| :---- | :---- |
| 1\. Preparation | Every role trains their specialty and unlocks their special skill |
| 2\. Know Your Spaceship | Mechanical packs spare parts and leads the systems puzzle. Doctor stocks the medical kit. Botanist packs seeds. Commander decides the cargo budget. Pilot leads the emergency drills |
| 3\. Launch | Pilot flies the ascent and the Mars burn. Commander gives the go or no-go. Mechanical runs the checklist. Doctor watches the crew’s health under launch forces |
| 4\. The Long Journey | Doctor tracks health. Botanist grows food. Mechanical repairs breakdowns. Pilot makes the mid-course correction. Commander handles the solar flare and morale |
| 5\. Survive on Mars | Pilot lands the ship. Botanist runs the greenhouse. Mechanical keeps the outpost working. Doctor protects morale. Commander makes the storm decisions |
| 6\. The Way Back Home | Pilot launches, docks, and re-enters. Mechanical rebuilds the filters. Doctor cares for the tired crew. Botanist feeds them. Commander makes the final calls |

### **Sample Script: Meeting the Crew**

**Capcom Jordan:** Let’s go around the room. Introduce yourselves, and tell me the one thing only you can do.

**Tomas:** Tomas Reyes, commander. I decide when we go and when we stop.

**Kenji:** Kenji, mechanical engineer. If it’s broken, I’ll fix it. If it’s not broken, I’ll tighten it anyway.

**Dr. Amara:** Dr. Okafor, medical officer. I’ll know you’re tired before you do.

**Priya:** Priya Nair, pilot. I make sure the ship goes where you point it, and lands where you hoped.

**Lucia:** Lucia Moreno, botanist. I grow the food, and I talk to the plants.

**Capcom Jordan:** Perfect. Mars isn’t going to know what hit it.

## **6\. How Levels Are Built**

* **Sub-levels** are short segments (3 to 8 minutes). Each teaches one idea.

* **Main missions** must be completed to move on.

* **Side missions** are optional and give bonus stars, upgrades, or story scenes.

* **Challenges** are special tests inside a mission. There are five types:

  1. **Budget:** split limited power, fuel, or weight.

  2. **Puzzle:** wire, route, or repair a system.

  3. **Timing:** act at the right moment.

  4. **Dilemma:** choose between two good or two bad options.

  5. **Quiz:** answer questions from what you learned.

## **7\. Level 1: Preparation**

**Goal:** Turn five ordinary people into a Mars crew.

**Learning outcome:** Space missions start years before launch, and training covers body, mind, and teamwork.

### **Opening narration**

*Four years before launch. A grey training hall, a long row of lockers, and a poster on the wall: “Mars is farther than you think.”*

### **Sub-levels**

**1.1 Fitness and Health** \- *Main mission:* Complete a training routine without exhausting the astronaut. \- *Challenge (Budget):* You have 10 training hours a week. Split them between strength, cardio, and rest. \- *Side mission:* Help Dr. Amara design a diet plan. \- *Fact:* In space, bones and muscles weaken without exercise.

**1.2 Space Basics Classroom** \- *Main mission:* Pass the “Mars 101” lesson. \- *Challenge (Quiz):* Questions on gravity, radiation, and Mars’s distance from Earth. \- *Side mission:* Find three hidden space facts in the classroom.

**1.3 Flight Simulator** \- *Main mission:* Land a practice capsule on a simulated Mars surface, with Priya coaching. \- *Challenge (Timing):* Fire thrusters at the right moments to slow down. Too early wastes fuel. Too late crashes.

**1.4 The Underwater Spacewalk** \- *Main mission:* Complete a repair task in a giant pool, which mimics weightlessness. \- *Challenge (Puzzle):* Move through a frame and tighten six bolts, with limited air.

**1.5 Team Test** \- *Main mission:* Survive a 3-day isolation test in a small room. \- *Challenge (Dilemma):* Two crew members disagree. Choose how to settle it.

**1.6 Role Training** \- *Main mission:* Complete one training task for each role: a repair for Kenji, triage for Dr. Amara, a landing for Priya, a crop plan for Lucia, and a hard call for Tomas. \- *Challenge (varies by role):* Each role has its own test, listed in Section 5\. \- *Side mission:* Learn a second role’s special skill at half strength (cross-training).

### **Sample script**

**Capcom Jordan:** Welcome to day one, crew. I’m not going to lie. Mars is hard. But hard is why we’re here.

**Tomas:** I have a question. What’s the one thing that ends missions early?

**Capcom Jordan:** Honestly? It’s usually not the rockets. It’s tired people making small mistakes.

**Dr. Amara:** Then we start by looking after each other.

**Priya:** And by not crashing the simulator.

**Capcom Jordan:** That too.

### **Ending line**

*The last test is done. On the wall, someone has added a line to the poster: “…but we’re ready.”*

## **8\. Level 2: Know Your Spaceship**

**Goal:** Learn every system on the ship, and pack it right.

**Learning outcome:** A spaceship is a set of connected systems, and each one needs power, air, and care.

### **Opening narration**

*The ship, named Meridian, floats on the launch pad. Inside, the walls are packed with switches and screens.*

### **Sub-levels**

**2.1 Ship Tour** \- *Main mission:* Visit the crew cabin, life support, engines, power, shield, and control room. Each stop shows what the module does. \- *Side mission:* Find the hidden emergency toolbox.

**2.2 Systems Puzzle** \- *Main mission:* Kenji connects solar panels, batteries, and life support so everything has power. \- *Challenge (Puzzle):* Route power through a circuit. If one path fails, an alternate must work.

**2.3 Emergency Drills** \- *Main mission:* Respond to three drills led by Priya: fire, air leak, and power failure. \- *Challenge (Timing):* Each drill has a countdown. Seal the right doors in the right order.

**2.4 Pack the Ship** \- *Main mission:* Load the cargo bay. \- *Challenge (Budget):* The ship can only carry 5,000 kg. Choose between food, water, spare parts, medical supplies, seeds, and personal items. \- *Side mission:* Pack a small item for each crew member that boosts morale later.

### **Sample script**

**Compass:** Welcome aboard. Every kilogram you add must be paid for in fuel.

**Kenji:** So every spare part is a trade-off.

**Compass:** Correct. Take too few, and a small failure becomes a disaster. Take too many, and the ship cannot lift off.

**Lucia:** And the seeds?

**Compass:** Seeds weigh almost nothing. I recommend them.

**Dr. Amara:** And the medical kit?

**Compass:** Non-negotiable.

### **Ending line**

*The cargo door seals. Kenji pats the hull. “Don’t worry. We packed everything we could.”*

## **9\. Level 3: Launch**

**Goal:** Leave Earth safely.

**Learning outcome:** A launch needs careful checks, perfect timing, and a precise burn to head for Mars.

### **Opening narration**

*Dawn on launch day. Fog over the sea. Five astronauts in orange suits climb into the capsule.*

### **Sub-levels**

**3.1 The Countdown Checklist** \- *Main mission:* Complete 10 checks before launch (fuel, seals, comms, suits). \- *Challenge (Puzzle):* One check hides a fault. Find it before the clock reaches zero. \- *Side mission:* Help Lucia calm a nervous crew member.

**3.2 Ascent** \- *Main mission:* Priya guides the rocket through the atmosphere. \- *Challenge (Timing):* Throttle down at “max-Q,” the moment of greatest air pressure on the rocket.

**3.3 Orbit and Mars Burn** \- *Main mission:* Reach Earth orbit, then fire the engine at exactly the right moment to leave for Mars. \- *Challenge (Timing and Budget):* Mars is only reachable during a launch window that opens about every 26 months. Burn too early or too late and the ship misses Mars. \- *Fact:* Planets move. Mission planners aim where Mars will be, not where it is.

### **Sample script**

**Capcom Jordan:** All stations report go. We are at T minus 30 seconds.

**Tomas:** Crew, this is it. Breathe.

**Priya:** Hands on the controls. Ready when you are, Commander.

**Capcom Jordan:** Ten, nine, eight…

**Lucia:** (whispering) Please let the seeds survive.

**Capcom Jordan:** …two, one, liftoff\! You are on your way.

### **Failure line**

**Capcom Jordan:** We’re holding. Everyone’s safe. We’ll fix it and try again tomorrow.

*A failed launch is never a crew loss. The player retries after fixing the mistake.*

## **10\. Level 4: The Long Journey**

**Goal:** Cross about 7 months of space and arrive in good shape.

**Learning outcome:** The trip itself is a challenge. Radiation, boredom, and equipment failure all test the crew.

### **Opening narration**

*Earth shrinks to a bright blue dot. Then it is just a star. The ship hums, and Tomas writes in his journal: “Day 1 of 210.”*

### **Sub-levels**

**4.1 Settling In** \- *Main mission:* Set a daily routine of work, exercise, meals, and sleep. \- *Challenge (Budget):* Divide a 16-hour day among tasks. Skip exercise and the crew weakens. Skip rest and mistakes follow.

**4.2 Mid-Course Correction** \- *Main mission:* Priya adjusts the ship’s path with a small engine burn. \- *Challenge (Timing and Budget):* Fuel is limited. A larger correction is safer but costs more fuel.

**4.3 Solar Flare** \- *Main mission:* Protect the crew from a radiation burst. \- *Challenge (Dilemma):* Shelter in the storm room (safe, but stops all work for a day) or keep working with extra shielding (riskier). \- *Fact:* In deep space, astronauts have far less protection than on the ground.

**4.4 Equipment Failure** \- *Main mission:* Repair a failing water recycler. \- *Challenge (Puzzle):* Kenji has a fault tree. Find the broken part by testing in the right order.

**4.5 The Quiet Weeks** \- *Main mission:* Keep morale up through months of isolation. \- *Challenge (Dilemma):* Capcom’s messages arrive delayed. Do you answer each one, or save power for emergencies? \- *Side mission:* Plan a birthday party using only ship supplies.

**4.6 Mars Ahead** \- *Main mission:* Prepare the ship for arrival and run final checks. \- *Challenge (Quiz):* A review of everything learned so far.

### **Sample script**

**Dr. Amara:** Day 90\. Everyone’s healthy, but I’m seeing early signs of boredom.

**Tomas:** Suggestions?

**Dr. Amara:** Give people something to look forward to. A movie night. A game. Anything.

**Compass:** Warning. Solar flare detected. Radiation levels will rise in 40 minutes.

**Tomas:** Everyone to the shelter. Now.

### **Ending line**

*A red dot grows ahead. For the first time in months, nobody’s talking. They’re just looking.*

## **11\. Level 5: Survive on Mars**

**Goal:** Land safely, build the outpost, and stay alive for 30 sols.

**Learning outcome:** Every system competes for limited power, and every choice has a cost.

This level uses the game already built, with the Meridian story. It is the main focus of the whole game. Every other level builds toward it, and its trade-offs return in every level.

### **Opening narration**

*Entry, descent, and landing takes about seven minutes. In those seven minutes, the ship goes from thousands of kilometers an hour to a gentle stop.*

### **Sub-levels**

**5.1 Landing** \- *Main mission:* Priya guides the lander through the thin Martian air. \- *Challenge (Timing):* Open the parachute, drop the heat shield, and fire the landing engines in order.

**5.2 Setting Up the Outpost** \- *Main mission:* Place the solar panels, life support, and greenhouse. \- *Challenge (Budget):* Only a certain amount of power is available during setup. \- *Side mission:* Find a safe landing marker for future crews.

**5.3 Sols 1 to 10: Learning the Rhythm** \- *Main mission:* Keep oxygen, water, food, and shield above zero. \- *Challenge (Budget):* The daily power allocation from the existing game. \- *Events:* Dust storms, solar flares, and leaks.

**5.4 Sols 11 to 20: Surprises** \- *Main mission:* Handle story events. \- *Story beats:* The fungus in the greenhouse, the old rover signal, and tired nerves. \- *Challenge (Dilemma):* Each beat has three choices with different effects.

**5.5 Sols 21 to 28: The Big Storm** \- *Main mission:* Prepare for and survive the solar storm. \- *Challenge (Dilemma):* Build the storm shelter or trust the main shield.

**5.6 Sols 29 and 30: Getting Ready to Leave** \- *Main mission:* Prepare the ascent vehicle and pack samples. \- *Challenge (Budget):* The ascent vehicle can carry only so much. Choose what comes home.

### **Sample script**

**Compass:** Parachute deployed. Altitude 8 kilometers.

**Priya:** Heat shield away.

**Capcom Jordan:** We lost your signal. We’ll see you on the other side.

**Compass:** Engines firing. Touchdown in 5, 4, 3…

**Lucia:** (laughing and crying) We’re here. We’re actually here.

## **12\. Level 6: The Way Back Home**

**Goal:** Leave Mars, find the ship, and return to Earth.

**Learning outcome:** Getting home is part of the mission, and it’s the hardest part.

### **Opening narration**

*The outpost goes quiet. Five astronauts stand in the red dust and look at the home they built. Then they climb aboard.*

### **Sub-levels**

**6.1 Launch from Mars** \- *Main mission:* Priya fires the ascent vehicle into orbit. \- *Challenge (Budget):* Mars has weaker gravity than Earth, so less fuel is needed. But the ascent vehicle only has enough for one attempt.

**6.2 Rendezvous** \- *Main mission:* Meet and dock with the transit ship waiting in orbit. \- *Challenge (Timing and Puzzle):* Match speed and line up the docking ports.

**6.3 The Return Cruise** \- *Main mission:* Fly home with worn-out equipment. \- *Challenge (Budget and Dilemma):* The air filters are failing. Kenji can repair them using parts saved from earlier levels, or the crew can reduce activity to save air. \- *Side mission:* Open the time capsule packed in Level 2\.

**6.4 Earth Entry** \- *Main mission:* Re-enter the atmosphere and splash down. \- *Challenge (Timing):* Hit the narrow entry window. Too steep burns up. Too shallow bounces off. \- *Fact:* Capsules reach about 28,000 km/h on re-entry before the parachutes open.

### **Sample script**

**Compass:** Docking complete. Welcome home, crew. Well, halfway home.

**Priya:** Smoothest dock I’ve ever flown, and nobody saw it.

**Dr. Amara:** I’ll check everyone. Then we sleep.

**Tomas:** Wait. Before we do, let’s say it out loud. Thank you all.

**Kenji:** For what?

**Tomas:** For not giving up.

### **Final scene**

*The parachutes bloom over the ocean. The capsule hits the waves, and the hatch opens to bright blue sky. Capcom Jordan’s voice, shaky: “Welcome home.”*

## **13\. Final Twist: The Message from the Moon**

After the last scene of Level 6, the player thinks the mission is over. It isn’t. A final scene plays after the credits, and it opens **Session 2**.

**The twist: Mission Control Jr. was never on Mars.** Mars was only one stop. The first outpost of the program is on the Moon, and it has just woken up.

### **When It Plays**

* It plays after the splashdown scene on every difficulty mode.

* The message is clearer on higher star ratings, but the twist is always revealed.

* It ends on a choice, which leads straight into Session 2\.

### **The Scene**

*Two days after splashdown. A quiet debrief room, with a window facing the sea. The crew is eating the first fresh food they’ve had in years.*

**Capcom Jordan:** Before we celebrate any more, I need to show you something. Compass picked it up on the way home and we kept it quiet until we were sure.

**Compass:** At 03:14 ship time, I detected a repeating signal from the far side of the Moon. The signal matches a registered ID. The ID is BA-0.

**Tomas:** Mission Control Jr. That’s the name of our program.

**Capcom Jordan:** It’s more than a name. It’s a base. A small one, built long ago at the Moon’s south pole, with a crew that was evacuated and a station that went dark.

**Kenji:** (slowly) The rover on Mars. The old one we found. The parts had a stamp on them. *BA-1.*

**Capcom Jordan:** Same program. Mars wasn’t the beginning. It was the second step.

**Compass:** Playing the message now.

**Recorded voice (Dr. Ilse Brandt, last commander of the first Mission Control Jr. base):** *“This is Mission Control Jr. If you’re hearing this, the base has power again. The night is long, but we left something for you. Come soon. The batteries won’t last another dark.”*

**Dr. Amara:** Why did it wake up now?

**Compass:** The solar storm during your stay on Mars also reached the Moon. It rebooted the base’s old systems.

**Lucia:** So the storm we feared turned the lights back on.

**Tomas:** How long do we have?

**Capcom Jordan:** The next lunar night begins in 14 days. The base won’t survive it without help.

### **The Cliffhanger Choice**

The scene ends with a choice. All three lead to Session 2, but they change how it starts.

| Choice | What it means | Effect on Session 2 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **“We go. Now.”** | The crew leaves for the Moon right away | Starts with less rest and less preparation, but more time before the night |
| **“Give us a week to prepare.”** | The crew trains and packs for the Moon | Starts with better supplies and full morale, but less time before the night |
| **“Send a robot team first.”** | Earth sends a scouting rover while the crew waits | Starts with a map of the base and its hazards, but a delayed landing |

### **How Earlier Choices Change the Twist**

## **16\. The Core Idea**

Session 1 teaches that every space mission is a chain. Training leads to a ship, the ship leads to a launch, the launch leads to a journey, and the journey leads to a new world and back. A weak link anywhere breaks the chain, and the player’s job is to look after every one.
